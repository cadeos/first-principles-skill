# Question toolkit

Pick the few that fit; don't run the whole list. In Coach mode, ask one at a time.

## 1. Clarification
- What exactly do you mean by "X"? Can you give a concrete example?
- What does success look like, in observable terms?
- Who is affected, and how would they notice?

## 2. Probe the assumption
- What must be true for this to be the case?
- Why do you believe that? Where did it come from?
- What if the opposite were true — what would change?
- Who decided it has to be this way, and for what reason?

## 3. Probe the evidence
- What supports this? How do you know?
- How strong is that evidence — measured, observed, or just repeated?
- What would change your mind?
- How often does this hold in general (base rate), not just in your case?

## 4. Alternative viewpoints
- How would an expert in a different field approach this?
- What would a complete beginner notice?
- What would a harsh critic say? What is the strongest version of the opposing view?
- Where has this problem already been solved (analogy, bright spots)?

## 5. Implications
- If this is true, what follows? And then what?
- What does believing this prevent you from considering?
- What does it cost to be wrong, and in which direction?

## 6. Meta
- Why is this the question being asked?
- What would be a better question?
- What is the real problem underneath this one?

## 7. Frame and headline (highest-yield, try these first)
- What two (or more) options does the question assume? What would both camps have to believe without evidence?
- What does this number / label actually consist of? Which component carries it?
- Is this a solution proposed before a diagnosis? What was the problem it was meant to solve?
- If the opposite framing were true, what would the question be?

## Reaching bedrock: the "why ladder"
Ask "why?" (or "how do you know?") repeatedly. Stop when the answer is one of:
- a physical/logical law,
- a measured fact,
- a stated requirement with an accountable owner,
- or "I don't know" (that is an unknown — record it, don't paper over it).

## Useful reframings
- **Reversal:** "What if the opposite of [assumption] were true?"
- **Outsider test:** "What would you tell a friend in this exact situation?"
- **Constraint removal:** "If [constraint] didn't exist, what would you do?"
- **Inversion:** "How would you guarantee failure?"
- **Null hypothesis:** "What if nothing changed? What does inaction cost?"
